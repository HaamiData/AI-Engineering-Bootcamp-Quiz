# Question 7: Median or Mean? The Effect of an Outlier

**Topics:** Descriptive Statistics, Outliers, Median, Mean

## 📝 Question

> The monthly salaries of 5 team members (in million tomans) are: 20, 22, 23, 25 and 110. Which measure is more appropriate for reporting the team's “typical salary”?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** The median (23), because it is not sensitive to the outlier 110 and gives a more typical picture.

**Option 2:** The mean (40), because it takes all data into account and is always the most representative number.

**Option 3:** The maximum (110), because it shows the maximum the team can afford to pay.

**Option 4:** 110 should be removed without any investigation and only the mean of the other four values should be reported.

## 💡 About this question

This question tests the choice of a **measure of central tendency** when the data contains an **outlier**.

Calculations: mean = `(20+22+23+25+110) / 5 = 200 / 5 = 40`; median (the middle value of the sorted data) = `23`.

The mean is pulled strongly by one very large value: 40 is higher than four of the five people, and nobody earns anything close to 40. The median is **robust** against outliers.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ✅ Correct

> The median (23), because it is not sensitive to the outlier 110 and gives a more typical picture.

**Correct.** The median (23) is not sensitive to the outlier 110 and better shows the "typical" salary; four of the five people earn between 20 and 25. For salaries, house prices and incomes, which have skewed distributions, the median is the more common report.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> The mean (40), because it takes all data into account and is always the most representative number.

**Incorrect.** The mean does take all data into account, but that is not always good; that is exactly why it shifts with an outlier. "Always the most representative number" is false. In this data, 40 describes no employee.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> The maximum (110), because it shows the maximum the team can afford to pay.

**Incorrect.** The maximum does not represent the "typical salary"; only one person earns it. The maximum is not a measure of central tendency, and one person's salary does not tell us about the "ability to pay" either.

### Option 4 — ❌ Incorrect

> 110 should be removed without any investigation and only the mean of the other four values should be reported.

**Incorrect.** Removing it without investigation is not sound practice. The value 110 may be a data-entry error or a real and important value (for example a senior executive). Its cause should be checked and the decision to keep or remove it made with a reason. It is better to report a robust measure (the median) and also mention the outlier.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 1**

> The median (23), because it is not sensitive to the outlier 110 and gives a more typical picture.

## 🎯 Why it is correct

A "typical salary" should reflect what most people earn. Because 110 skews the data heavily, the median (23) serves that goal better and the mean (40) is misleading.

## 📌 Key takeaway

For data with outliers or skewed distributions, prefer the median over the mean, and investigate the outlier rather than blindly deleting it.
