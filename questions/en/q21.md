# Question 21: Probability of Disease Given a Positive Test (Bayes' Theorem)

**Topics:** Bayes' Theorem, Sensitivity and Specificity, Base Rate, False Positives

## 📝 Question

> A test for a disease has 95% sensitivity and 95% specificity. The prevalence of the disease in the population is 1%. A person tests positive; approximately what is the probability that they are actually sick?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** 50%

**Option 2:** 95%

**Option 3:** 5%

**Option 4:** About 16%

## 💡 About this question

This question tests **base rate neglect**. A sensitivity of 95% means `P(positive | sick) = 0.95`; but the question asks for `P(sick | positive)`, which is something different and depends on the prevalence.

Solving with natural frequencies on 10,000 people:

- Sick: 1% = **100 people** → test positive: `0.95 × 100 = 95` people
- Healthy: 9,900 people → false positives (1 − specificity = 5%): `0.05 × 9900 = 495` people
- Total positives: `95 + 495 = 590`
- Probability of being sick given a positive test: `95 / 590 ≈ 0.161`, i.e. **about 16%**

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ❌ Incorrect

> 50%

**Incorrect.** 50% comes from "guessing" or ignoring the prevalence. That number would be right if the numbers of sick and healthy people were equal (50% prevalence), not 1%.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> 95%

**Incorrect.** 95% is the sensitivity, i.e. `P(positive | sick)`, not `P(sick | positive)`. Confusing these two probabilities is a common fallacy (known as the prosecutor's fallacy). With low prevalence, most positives come from healthy people.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> 5%

**Incorrect.** 5% is the false-positive rate among healthy people (`1 − specificity`), not the probability of disease given a positive test. The real probability of disease is higher than 5%, because about 95 true positives exist among 590 positives.

### Option 4 — ✅ Correct

> About 16%

**Correct.** Of the 590 people who test positive, only 95 are truly sick: `95/590 ≈ 16%`. Because the disease is rare, the number of false positives from healthy people (495) exceeds the true positives (95).

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 4**

> About 16%

## 🎯 Why it is correct

`P(sick | positive) = (0.95×0.01) / (0.95×0.01 + 0.05×0.99) ≈ 0.16`. Low prevalence makes even a 95%-accurate test generate many false positives.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Always consider the base rate; high sensitivity and specificity alone do not tell you the probability of disease after a positive test.
