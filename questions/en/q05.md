# Question 5: Comparing Two Models on 100 Test Samples

**Topics:** Model Evaluation, Statistical Significance, Test-Set Size, Accuracy vs AUC

## 📝 Question

> Model A and Model B were evaluated on the same 100 test samples; A got 90% accuracy and B got 88%. Which conclusion is more reasonable?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** Model B is better, because lower accuracy indicates that it has overfit less and performs better on new data.

**Option 2:** Accuracy is an invalid metric for comparing models and one should always compare using only AUC, because AUC is more reliable in every situation.

**Option 3:** Model A is better and should replace B, because both were tested under identical conditions and A got a higher accuracy.

**Option 4:** The difference equals 2 samples out of 100, and with this amount of data we cannot confidently conclude that one is truly better.

## 💡 About this question

This question tests **statistical significance** when comparing models on a small test set. A higher number does not automatically mean a better model; we have to ask whether the difference is larger than random fluctuation.

A few calculations for intuition:

- 90% on 100 samples means 90 correct and 88% means 88 correct; the **difference = 2 samples**.
- The standard error of accuracy for A is about `√(0.9×0.1/100) = 0.03`, i.e. 3 percentage points; the 95% confidence interval is roughly 84% to 96%. For B it is roughly 82% to 94%. The two intervals overlap heavily.
- Since both models were evaluated on the same samples, a paired test (such as McNemar's) is more appropriate; but with a net difference of only 2 samples, even that test does not give a significant result.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ❌ Incorrect

> Model B is better, because lower accuracy indicates that it has overfit less and performs better on new data.

**Incorrect.** Overfitting is measured by the gap between training and test performance, not by lower test accuracy. Lower accuracy on the test set is not by itself a sign of "less overfitting"; Model B may simply be weaker. The argument is logically unfounded (and in any case a 2-point difference is not significant).

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> Accuracy is an invalid metric for comparing models and one should always compare using only AUC, because AUC is more reliable in every situation.

**Incorrect.** Accuracy is not an "invalid" metric; it just has limitations (for example, it can be misleading on imbalanced data). AUC is also not more reliable "in every situation": it ignores the operating point and calibration, and with severely imbalanced data it can look optimistic. Words like "always" and "in every situation" signal an extreme claim. Besides, even AUC would not fix the small-sample problem.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> Model A is better and should replace B, because both were tested under identical conditions and A got a higher accuracy.

**Incorrect.** Identical test conditions make the comparison *fair*, but not *significant*. With 100 samples, a 2-sample difference is within random fluctuation. Replacing a model also requires looking beyond accuracy at cost, latency, stability and other criteria. This option draws a firm conclusion from a negligible difference.

### Option 4 — ✅ Correct

> The difference equals 2 samples out of 100, and with this amount of data we cannot confidently conclude that one is truly better.

**Correct.** The difference is 2 samples out of 100, and the confidence intervals of the two models overlap heavily; with this amount of data we cannot say with confidence that one is truly better. For a valid conclusion you need a larger test set, cross-validation or a proper confidence interval / statistical test, and a look at other metrics too.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 4**

> The difference equals 2 samples out of 100, and with this amount of data we cannot confidently conclude that one is truly better.

## 🎯 Why it is correct

A difference of 2 samples out of 100 is within statistical noise. A 90% vs 88% accuracy gap on this amount of data is not enough evidence that one model is better; so the most careful and correct conclusion is option 4.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Before declaring a winner, check sample size and uncertainty (confidence intervals, statistical tests); a small numeric gap on a small test set is usually just noise.
