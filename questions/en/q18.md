# Question 18: Evaluating the Claim of “Completely Eliminating Hallucination”

**Topics:** Hallucination, Top-K, Temperature, Absolute Claims

## 📝 Question

> A user asked an AI assistant to propose a solution for eliminating hallucination in a RAG chatbot. The assistant's answer: “I applied three changes: 1) raised Top-K from 3 to 20 so more information reaches the model; 2) set Temperature to 0 so the output is deterministic; 3) wrote in the prompt ‘answer only based on the retrieved text’. With these three changes, hallucination is completely eliminated.” Which evaluation of this answer is more accurate?

## 🔢 Options

**Option 1:** Temperature 0 has absolutely no effect on hallucination; therefore the second change is wrong and the other two are correct.

**Option 2:** Increasing Top-K from 3 to 20 always improves quality, and the prompt instruction guarantees that the model uses only the text.

**Option 3:** Writing an instruction in the prompt has no effect on the model's behavior, and the only way to reduce hallucination is to Fine-Tune the model.

**Option 4:** The claim of ‘complete elimination’ is incorrect; these changes only reduce the likelihood of errors, and greatly increasing Top-K can even worsen quality by adding noise to the context.

## 💡 About this question

This question is a **critique of an absolute claim**. All three of the assistant's changes are useful or can be useful, but none guarantees anything. The right view: each change has a probable, not certain, effect, and a claim like "completely eliminated" about hallucination is almost always false.

The effect of each: a **larger Top-K** may include the right document more often (recall) but increases noise and latency; **Temperature 0** reduces random variation but a consistent mistake is still possible; a **prompt instruction** changes behavior but is not guaranteed.

## 🔍 Detailed review of every option

### Option 1 — ❌ Incorrect

> Temperature 0 has absolutely no effect on hallucination; therefore the second change is wrong and the other two are correct.

**Incorrect.** Temperature 0 is not "completely ineffective": it reduces sampling randomness and can reduce some errors (and improves consistency). So calling it "wrong" is a mistake. Also, "the other two are correct" is false, because a claim of complete elimination is not defensible with any of them.

### Option 2 — ❌ Incorrect

> Increasing Top-K from 3 to 20 always improves quality, and the prompt instruction guarantees that the model uses only the text.

**Incorrect.** "Always improves quality" is false: a large Top-K can bring in irrelevant chunks and distract the model. And a prompt instruction does not "guarantee" anything; the model may use its internal knowledge or misread the chunks.

### Option 3 — ❌ Incorrect

> Writing an instruction in the prompt has no effect on the model's behavior, and the only way to reduce hallucination is to Fine-Tune the model.

**Incorrect.** A prompt instruction **does** affect model behavior (for example requiring answers based on the text and allowing "I don't know"). Fine-tuning is also not the only way; better retrieval, re-ranking, guardrails, citation and output verification also reduce hallucination.

### Option 4 — ✅ Correct

> The claim of ‘complete elimination’ is incorrect; these changes only reduce the likelihood of errors, and greatly increasing Top-K can even worsen quality by adding noise to the context.

**Correct.** These changes only reduce the **likelihood** of errors; they do not eliminate hallucination. A greatly increased Top-K can also make quality worse by adding irrelevant or conflicting chunks (noise and important information getting lost in the context). That is the accurate evaluation: helpful but insufficient for a "complete elimination" claim.

## ✅ Correct answer

**Option 4**

> The claim of ‘complete elimination’ is incorrect; these changes only reduce the likelihood of errors, and greatly increasing Top-K can even worsen quality by adding noise to the context.

## 🎯 Why it is correct

Hallucination cannot be "eliminated" with a few settings; it can only be reduced and monitored. The absolute claim and the "always better" claim about Top-K are both incorrect.

## 📌 Key takeaway

Be suspicious of absolute claims (“completely”, “always”, “guaranteed”) and measure each change's effect with evaluation.
